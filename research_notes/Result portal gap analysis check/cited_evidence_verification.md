## Q5. Technical sources — correct bibliographic details

### Takeaway
Four of the URLs resolve to genuine peer-reviewed papers and should be cited formally. One is mis-described: the EURECOM PDF is **not** a survey of flash-crowd techniques. "Baroro" and "stackharbor" could not be found and should be dropped. The mojoauth and oneuptime items are vendor blogs: keep them only as grey literature, or replace them.

### Cited Findings
- **EURECOM ce-felbpa-041018.pdf — mis-described (Contradicted if the plan calls it a survey or "flash crowd techniques" overview).** It is EURECOM publication 1489: **P. Felber, T. Kaldewey, S. Weiss, "Proactive Hot Spot Avoidance for Web Server Dependability," Proc. 23rd IEEE Int. Symp. on Reliable Distributed Systems (SRDS 2004), Florianópolis, Brazil, 18–20 Oct 2004.** It proposes collaborative on-the-fly replication of hot content among small and medium web servers with request redirection, implemented as an Apache module — [EURECOM record](https://www.eurecom.edu/en/publication/1489); [PDF](https://www.eurecom.fr/publication/1489/download/ce-felbpa-041018.pdf); [SRDS 2004 listing](https://rankdblp.com/venues/db_conf_srds_srds2004_html.html). (Page numbers and DOI not verified.)
- **Wendell & Freedman — Confirmed, but the plan cites the *slides* URL.** The correct citation is **P. Wendell, M. J. Freedman, "Going Viral: Flash Crowds in an Open CDN," Proc. ACM Internet Measurement Conference (IMC '11), Berlin, Nov 2011, pp. 549–558, DOI 10.1145/2068816.2068867.** Note that Wendell was at UC Berkeley and Freedman at Princeton. The paper analyses four or more years of CoralCDN traffic (over 33 billion HTTP requests) and thousands of flash crowds. Its findings include that cache cooperation helped substantially in about 40% of crowds and that resources would need to spin up in under a minute — [paper PDF](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~mfreed/docs/flash-imc11.pdf); [Princeton record](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/going-viral-flash-crowds-in-an-open-cdn/); [slides](https://sns.cs.princeton.edu/assets/slides/2011-imc-wendell.pdf)

Shimokawa, "Preliminary Study on World-Wide Implementation of Adaptive Content Distribution Network," Electronic Communications of the EASST, vol. 37 (WowKiVS 2011), 2011, DOI 10.14279/tuj.eceasst.37.488.** FCAN (Flash Crowds Alleviation Network) dynamically switches between client-server and P2P cache-proxy structures. The original FCAN paper is by C. Pan, M. Atajanov, M. B. Hossain, T. Shimokawa, N. Yoshida in *IEICE Transactions* (exact volume and year not verified here) — [ECEASST](https://eceasst.org/index.php/eceasst/article/view/2398); [TU Berlin mirror](https://journal.ub.tu-berlin.de/eceasst/article/view/488); [IEICE author page](https://global.ieice.org/en_transactions/Author/a_name=Chenyu%20PAN)

### Inferences
- For an evidence table, rank by source strength. Tier A (official statement or major outlet): Maharashtra SSC 2021, UCAS 2011, Quebec 2026, MPBSE 2026 (FPJ). Tier B (single specialist outlet): CBSE 2017, JEE Adv 2016, Anna Univ 2021, Bihar 2026, KCET 2026, Re-NEET 2026. Tier C (low-tier aggregators): Uttarakhand 2026, CUET 2026.
- Recurring patterns that support the thesis: (a) crashes happen at a scheduled, publicly announced instant (a synchronised flash crowd); (b) boards fall back to mirrors, SMS, DigiLocker and WhatsApp, which is ad-hoc load-shedding; (c) failures sometimes sit in auxiliary services such as authentication (Quebec) rather than the result lookup itself.
- The lack of published traffic telemetry from NIC, CBSE or DigiLocker is itself a finding: the thesis can argue that public capacity data is missing. An RTI request or direct contact with NIC is a possible route, but it was not attempted.
