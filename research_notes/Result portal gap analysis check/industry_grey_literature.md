**Bottom line for novelty:** none of the sources found **measures and compares** DB-backed vs cache-aside vs precomputed static/KV vs precomputed + waiting room for a result-day workload. Practitioners publish (a) single-architecture success stories with headline peaks, (b) vendor before/after claims for waiting rooms, and (c) incident statements. The thesis's "no measured comparison exists" claim holds against what this search turned up, but the claim should be worded as "no published comparison found" and should cite the sources above as the practitioner baseline.

## Q1. How do Indian result portals actually run, and are traffic numbers published?

### Takeaway
No official NIC, CBSE, NTA or state-board account of result-portal architecture or result-day traffic (RPS, concurrent users) was found. Public information is limited to the delivery channels (multiple .nic.in mirrors, DigiLocker, UMANG, SMS/IVRS, state apps) and widespread news and blog reports of slowdowns and crashes. The closest Indian numbers with an official source come from analogues: CoWIN (2.7M hits/min) and IRCTC (30,155 bookings/min).

### Cited Findings
- CBSE results are spread across several official channels: CBSE result websites (results.cbse.nic.in, cbseresults.nic.in, cbse.gov.in), results.digilocker.gov.in, the UMANG app, SMS and IVRS. Guides present DigiLocker and UMANG as backups when the main site slows down. These are blog/SEO pages, not CBSE statements. — [PW Live](https://www.pw.live/news/cbse-board-result-2026-class-10-12-date-expected-april-websites-how-to-check); [studyless.in](https://studyless.in/why-is-the-cbse-results-website-down/)
- One 2026 report says more than 25 lakh (2.5M) students checked CBSE Class 10 marks at once across CBSE's site, DigiLocker and UMANG on the 15 Apr 2026 release. This is a student count, not a request rate, and it comes from a guide site (snippet-level). — [collegesimplified.in](https://www.collegesimplified.in/post/how-to-use-digilocker-to-download-cbse-class-10-result-2026)
- Claims that DigiLocker "handles traffic via dedicated cloud structures" and that UMANG "handles peak traffic efficiently" come from blogs with no data behind them. — [tutoroot](https://www.tutoroot.com/blog/cbse-result-on-digilocker/); [studyless.in](https://studyless.in/why-is-the-cbse-results-website-down/)
- NIC's National Cloud (MeghRaj) catalogue lists a load-balancer service that spreads traffic across back-end server pools. No result-specific use is documented. — [NIC National Cloud LB](https://cloud.nicsi.nic.in/user/services_lb.php); [NIC National Cloud](https://www.nic.gov.in/service/national-cloud/)
- NTA: guides point to DigiLocker and a backup portal (results.nta.ac.in) on result days, and describe 503s and login failures. No official NTA capacity or cloud statement was found. — [Careers360](https://medicine.careers360.com/articles/re-neet-2026-result-server-issue); [Tribune (NTA site crash, undated)](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/jalandhar/wait-gets-longer-for-students-parents-as-nta-website-crashes-157006)
- Kerala SSLC uses several official portals and apps on result day: results.kite.kerala.gov.in, keralaresults.nic.in, prd.kerala.gov.in, results.kerala.gov.in, sslcexam.kerala.gov.in, the PRD Live app and KITE's Saphalam app. This is an explicit **mirror / multi-origin** strategy, but no traffic numbers are published. — [Careers360 2023](https://news.careers360.com/kerala-sslc-result-2023-live-updates-class-10th-results-announced-keralaresults-nic-in-how-to-check-direct-link-toppers-news/amp); [Xylem](https://xylemlearning.com/kerala-sslc-result-2026/)
- Maharashtra HSC 2026: a commercial blog reports that lakhs of students access results at once and that mahresult slows down or crashes. Low reliability. — [Target Publications](https://targetpublications.org/blog/maharashtra-hsc-result-2026-alternative-websites-mahresult-crash)
- CoWIN (Indian Govt scheduled surge, 28 Apr 2021): the Health Ministry denied a server crash. It reported 383 million API hits, with up to 2.7 million hits per minute in the first three hours, and ~1.33 crore registrations on day 1. Users still reported OTP failures and crashes. — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/80-lakh-register-for-covid-19-vaccination-in-first-three-hours-on-wednesday-980206.html); [Scroll](https://scroll.in/latest/993552/cowin-aarogya-setu-crash-minutes-after-covid-vaccine-registration-opens-for-all-adults-in-india); [The Quint](https://www.thequint.com/coronavirus/cowin-and-aarogya-setu-down-citizens-complain-on-day-1-of-vaccine-registration)
- CoWIN later limited public API access to 100 calls per 5 minutes per IP, after slot-tracker bots were built. This is evidence of automated-polling amplification in an Indian scheduled surge. — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/infocus/covid19-tech-impact/startupsvscovid19-govts-limits-on-cowin-apis-hamstrings-third-party-vaccine-slot-trackers/)
- IRCTC official per-minute record: 30,155 bookings at 10:01 AM on 12 Mar 2025. — [IRCTC](https://www.irctc.com/internet-ticketing.php). Reported upgrade capacity: 150,000 bookings/min and >4M enquiries/min, up from ~32,000 and ~400,000. The rollout date conflicts between sources. — [The420.in](https://the420.in/irctc-new-website-launch-tatkal-booking-capacity/). An unofficial figure of 37,410 tickets/min (16 Aug 2025) is unverified. — [systemdesign.academy](https://www.systemdesign.academy/interview/design-irctc). A Medium post claims 0.8–1.0M users try to log in during the 15-minute Tatkal window and ~330–350k get through (unverified). — [Medium](https://medium.com/thesystemdesign/how-irctc-handles-crores-of-users-during-tatkal-booking-f5209531c5cd)
- Hotstar 2019 (AWS re:Invent CMY302): 25.3M concurrent viewers. The talk covers pre-warming before match time, scaling on request count or concurrency rather than CPU, and the HULK load-generation and "Tsunami" test framework. The deck attributes 25.3M to the IPL 2019 Final, while other sources attribute it to the 2019 World Cup India–NZ semi-final, so the event is unresolved. — [AWS re:Invent deck](https://d1.awsstatic.com/events/reinvent/2019/Scaling_Hotstar.com_for_25_million_concurrent_viewers_CMY302.pdf); [Neo Kim newsletter](https://newsletter.systemdesign.one/p/hotstar-scaling). Secondary write-ups report reactive ASG scaling could not keep up with ~1.1M users/minute growth (snippet-level). — [techlogstack](https://techlogstack.com/explore/hotstar-ipl-scaling-2019/)

### Inferences
- Indian boards already use **channel diversification and mirrors** (multiple .nic.in and state domains, DigiLocker, UMANG, SMS, apps) as a de facto load-spreading strategy. This multi-origin design is not among the thesis's four architectures and could be cited as the "status quo" baseline or added as a variant.
- With no official result-day RPS published, the thesis's workload model has to be built from analogues: KNEC's 300k hits/s claim, CoWIN's 2.7M hits/min (~45k hits/s), IRCTC's per-minute records and Hotstar's concurrency curves. Candidate counts (e.g., 25 lakh CBSE Class 10) combined with assumed refresh rates are the other route. This itself is a contribution point.
- Hotstar's "scale on concurrency, pre-warm before the scheduled event" practice supports a precomputed / pre-provisioned design for a known release time.

### Gaps
- No NIC Informatics magazine article, MeitY note or NIC case study with result-day RPS, cache-hit ratio or CDN use was found. Akamai or Cloudflare use by Indian boards could not be confirmed. A DNS/HTTP header check on results.cbse.nic.in and keralaresults.nic.in on a result day would settle this, but it is outside the scope of desk research.
- No public information was found on private vendors (indiaresults.com, examresults.net) and their architecture or traffic.
- No NTA statement on cloud hosting (AWS/Azure/MeghRaj) for JEE/NEET/CUET results was found.
- Searches turned up no LinkedIn or blog post by "Eshaan Sharma" on result-day precompute + KV + waiting room design. The only match was an unrelated LinkedIn profile. — [LinkedIn search result](https://www.linkedin.com/in/eshaansharmaa/). If the thesis cites this post, the exact URL must come from the student.

## Q2. Cloud vendor / national case studies on exam result days (India or elsewhere) with numbers

### Takeaway
No AWS, Azure or GCP case study with numbers for an exam-results-day portal was found. Published numbers on exam result surges come from exam bodies' own incident statements (KNEC Kenya: "up to 300,000 hits per second") and old UK reporting (UCAS traffic quadrupled in 2011). These are incident claims, not architecture comparisons.

### Cited Findings
- KNEC (Kenya), 9 Jan 2025, KCSE 2024 results: the portal went down soon after release. KNEC said on X: "The minor delays are due to the high number of requests, with up to 300,000 hits per second." KNEC had moved from SMS to the online portal after fake SMS results in earlier years. — [The Standard](https://thestandard.ke/business/education/article/2001509305/knec-denies-website-down-as-candidates-unable-to-access-results); [The Star](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/2025-01-09-knec-website-crashes-hours-after-release-of-kcse); [Kenya Times](https://thekenyatimes.com/breaking-news/knec-issues-update-after-kcse-2024-results-portal-crashes/); [Capital FM](https://www.capitalfm.co.ke/news/2025/01/knec-portal-crashes-moments-after-release-of-2024-kcse-results/)
- The KNEC portal crashed again for KCSE 2025 results (Jan 2026). — [Kenyans.co.ke](https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/119687-knec-portal-crashes-thousands-kenyans-rush-access-2025-kcse-results)
- Nigeria: WAEC temporarily suspended its result portal after post-processing glitches, and a JAMB glitch affected several states (2025). These were data-correctness failures, not load failures. — [AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202508270098.html)
- UCAS (UK): in 2011, Track was down for two hours when traffic quadrupled. UCAS then moved critical services to a cloud environment with "tested capacity several times the expected peaks". No absolute numbers were given. — [Times Higher Education](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/ucas-says-cloud-will-keep-it-safe-from-results-day-storm/2005982.article)
- UK Clearing: Cirrus, a contact-centre platform on AWS supporting universities, sees call volumes ~10x normal during Clearing, with some universities receiving up to 7,500 calls/day. This is a voice channel, not web. — [Softcat case study](https://www.softcat.com/about-us/case-studies/softcat-aws-support-ensures-straight-as-cirrus-exam-results-day)
- Bangladesh SSC/HSC: results are published via educationboardresults.gov.bd and eboardresults.com plus Teletalk SMS. Teletalk runs a "Result Publication System". No traffic numbers were found. — [Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/education/386011/how-to-check-ssc-results); [Wikipedia: Teletalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teletalk)
- Fetch (AWS case study, Super Bowl 2025): 2.67M requests/s peak served using CloudFront with Origin Shield to cut origin load, plus daily stress tests. This is the clearest vendor-published number for a **scheduled, CDN-fronted** surge. — [AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/solutions/case-studies/fetch-case-study)

### Inferences
- KNEC's "300,000 hits/s" is a useful order-of-magnitude anchor for a national results release (~900k candidates in Kenya). It is the exam body's own unaudited claim and may count every HTTP object rather than result lookups, so cite it with that caveat.
- The KNEC switch from SMS to web (and the re-crash a year later) is a concrete example of a change of delivery channel that increased web load without a published architecture change.

### Gaps
- No Azure or Google Cloud case study for an exam board was found. Nothing was found for Philippines, Sri Lanka or Pakistan boards (not specifically searched due to budget).
- The Southampton tech-team blog post "exam results release" (2014) may describe a university results-release architecture, but the host could not be fetched in this session. — [blog.soton.ac.uk](https://blog.soton.ac.uk/techteam/2014/06/27/exam-results-release/)

## Q3. Waiting rooms (Queue-it, Cloudflare, AWS) for exams/results/admissions and published measurements

### Takeaway
Waiting rooms are documented for university registration and vaccine booking, not for exam-results release. The only "measurements" are vendor-reported before/after business metrics (support tickets, scaling cost) or capacity-planning examples. No source published fairness metrics, origin-load reduction curves or a comparison against non-queued designs.

### Cited Findings
- Queue-it, universities: LSE used a managed queue to limit concurrent students on a system with known code issues. King's College London protected a housing-registration portal and reported an 80% reduction in support requests. Queue-it also reports a 38% decrease in server-scaling costs and a 51% decrease in on-call staff (vendor marketing). — [Queue-it blog](https://queue-it.com/blog/university-registrations-online-queue/)
- Cloudflare Waiting Room: offers FIFO, random and lottery queueing. Cloudflare's engineering blog describes how queueing decisions are coordinated across its distributed network (not fetched in this session). — [Cloudflare product page](https://www.cloudflare.com/en-gb/application-services/products/waiting-room/); [Cloudflare blog: How Waiting Room makes queueing decisions](https://blog.cloudflare.com/how-waiting-room-queues/); [Cloudflare blog: random queueing](https://blog.cloudflare.com/waiting-room-random-queueing-and-custom-web-mobile-apps/)
- Verto Health (Cloudflare case study): used Waiting Room for COVID-19 vaccine scheduling, with surges lasting 1–2 hours and over 66 million requests in one month. — [Cloudflare](https://www.cloudflare.com/case-studies/verto/)
- BookMyShow reportedly configured a Cloudflare waiting room for 10,000 concurrent users. This comes from a third-party write-up (snippet-level, low reliability). — [Medium](https://medium.com/@chiragshetty98/demystifying-cloudflares-waiting-room-db589f2be6f6)
- AWS Virtual Waiting Room on AWS (open-source solution, 2022): its sizing example is an event lasting two hours where the site can process 50 users/s but 250/s are expected. This is a worked example, not a benchmark. — [AWS docs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/solutions/latest/aws-virtual-waiting-room/welcome.html); [AWS Compute Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/compute/introducing-aws-virtual-waiting-room/)
- SeatGeek: waiting room built with API Gateway, Lambda and DynamoDB around a token service. — [AWS Architecture Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/architecture/build-a-virtual-waiting-room-with-amazon-dynamodb-and-aws-lambda-at-seatgeek/)
- Open-source design: vercel-labs/nextjs-waiting-room (FIFO, atomic admission, fail-open circuit breaker, sorted set keyed by session expiry). — [GitHub](https://github.com/vercel-labs/nextjs-waiting-room/blob/main/docs/waiting-room-scale-architecture.md)
- Academic-adjacent: a 2024 Radboud BSc thesis implements a distributed virtual waiting room and reports throughput testing, including a non-simulated deployment (numbers not retrieved). — [Radboud thesis PDF](https://www.cs.ru.nl/bachelors-theses/2024/Josja_Koopmans___1071047___Distributed_Virtual_Waiting_Room.pdf)
- Artillery's GitHub discussion shows practitioners struggling to load-test through a Cloudflare waiting room. This is relevant to the thesis's method for measuring the waiting-room architecture. — [GitHub discussion](https://github.com/artilleryio/artillery/discussions/3325); Cloudflare's own testing doc: [Test a waiting room](https://developers.cloudflare.com/waiting-room/additional-options/test-waiting-room/)

### Inferences
- The thesis's fourth design (precompute + waiting room) is well supported by practice: waiting rooms are a mature commodity (Queue-it, Cloudflare, AWS solution, OSS). However, no published source quantifies **whether a waiting room adds value when the backend is already a precomputed static/KV store**. That is a defensible gap.
- Vendor metrics (support tickets, on-call staff) are not system metrics. A thesis measuring p99 latency, error rate, origin RPS and time-to-result under the same replayed surge would add something new.

### Gaps
- No documented exam-results deployment of Queue-it, Cloudflare Waiting Room or the AWS VWR solution was found.
- No published fairness measurement (e.g., order preservation, wait-time distribution) from a production waiting room was found. Cloudflare's blog may contain design-level discussion but could not be fetched.

## Q4. Scheduled-surge analogues with post-mortems: do any compare architectures or quantify retry amplification?

### Takeaway
Post-mortems and official statements exist for HealthCare.gov (2013), the Australian eCensus (2016), CoWIN (2021) and Ticketmaster Eras Tour (2022). They report peak load against expectation, and some causes, but **none compares alternative architectures**. Retry and bot amplification appears only qualitatively or as aggregate multiples (Ticketmaster: 4x previous peak, partly blamed on bots and fans without codes).

### Cited Findings
- Ticketmaster Eras Tour (Nov 2022): 3.5 billion total system requests, "4x our previous peak", attributed to bot attacks and fans without codes. 3.5M Verified Fan registrations; 1.5M invited, 2M waitlisted (invites sized on "40% show up, ~3 tickets each"); >2M tickets sold on 15 Nov. — [Variety](https://variety.com/2022/music/news/ticketmaster-explains-taylor-swift-ticket-crisis-eras-tour-1235435673/); [Ticketmaster statement](https://business.ticketmaster.com/press-release/taylor-swift-the-eras-tour-onsale-explained/). A claimed prior peak of ~875M and Senate testimony details are unverified. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/gabrielanhaia/design-ticketmaster-for-a-35-billion-request-presale-waiting-room-seat-holds-expiry-1fck)
- HealthCare.gov (Oct 2013): 50–60k simultaneous users expected, up to 250,000 seen (CTO Todd Park). A testing bulletin from the day before launch said the system could reach 1,100 users before response time got too high. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/11/07/healthcare-gov-could-only-handle-1100-users-on-the-eve-of-launch); [House Oversight](https://oversight.house.gov/release/aca-testing-bulletin-healthcare-gov-handle-1100-users-day-launch)
- Australian eCensus (9 Aug 2016): load-tested for ~250 forms/s (~900,000/hour). Real load tracked predictions and was "well within capacity". The outage (nearly two days) was due to DDoS, IBM's failure to deliver contracted DDoS protection, Vocus geoblocking misconfiguration and a mis-configured router. — [ParlInfo MacGibbon review](https://parlinfo.aph.gov.au/parlInfo/search/display/display.w3p;query=Id:%22publications/tabledpapers/a41f4f25-a08e-49a7-9b5f-d2c8af94f5c5%22); [iTnews](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/geoblocking-wasnt-enough-to-secure-census-macgibbon-440081); [Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/450403576/IBM-blamed-for-Australian-census-website-crash)
- CoWIN (2021): 2.7M hits/min peak and 383M hits in 3 hours. The government denied a crash while users reported failures. API throttling to 100 calls / 5 min / IP was introduced against slot-tracker bots, an implicit acknowledgment of automated polling load. — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/80-lakh-register-for-covid-19-vaccination-in-first-three-hours-on-wednesday-980206.html); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/infocus/covid19-tech-impact/startupsvscovid19-govts-limits-on-cowin-apis-hamstrings-third-party-vaccine-slot-trackers/)
- Hotstar (2019): reactive autoscaling was judged too slow, so the team pre-warmed and pre-provisioned for scheduled match times and ran large-scale "Tsunami" load tests that replayed realistic user journeys. — [AWS re:Invent CMY302](https://d1.awsstatic.com/events/reinvent/2019/Scaling_Hotstar.com_for_25_million_concurrent_viewers_CMY302.pdf)

### Inferences
- These cases support the thesis's workload model on two points: (1) actual peaks routinely exceed planned capacity by 4–5x (HealthCare.gov ~250k vs 50–60k; Ticketmaster 4x prior peak), and (2) automated or impatient clients (bots, refresh, slot trackers) inflate request volume beyond the human population. None publishes a retry-amplification factor, so the thesis's modelling of retries/refresh as a parameter would be novel relative to this grey literature.
- The eCensus case is a counter-example worth citing: capacity planning matched demand, and the failure came from the network and security layer. A result-portal evaluation that ignores DDoS and bot traffic misses a real failure mode.

## Q5. Open-source result-portal projects / benchmarks, and peer-reviewed flash-crowd measurement

### Takeaway
GitHub repository searches for "exam result portal load test" and "board result portal scalability" returned **zero** repositories. The closest existing measured workloads are classic peer-reviewed flash-crowd traces (World Cup '98, Slashdot, CoralCDN), not exam results.

### Cited Findings
- GitHub repository search for "exam result portal load test" returned 0 results, and "board result portal scalability" returned 0 results (GitHub API, Oct 2026). Source: the session's GitHub search tool; no URL.
- The only relevant OSS artifacts found were waiting-room implementations: [vercel-labs/nextjs-waiting-room](https://github.com/vercel-labs/nextjs-waiting-room/blob/main/docs/waiting-room-scale-architecture.md) and the AWS Virtual Waiting Room solution ([AWS docs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/solutions/latest/aws-virtual-waiting-room/welcome.html)).
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Chen & Heidemann, flash-crowd mitigation via adaptive admission control, using Slashdot and 1998 World Cup HTTP logs. The mean request interval was ~1 s in the Slashdot trace and <10 ms in the World Cup trace. — [ACM TOIT PDF](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/1084772.1084776); [ISI PDF](https://ant.isi.edu/~johnh/PAPERS/Chen03b.pdf)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Arlitt & Jin's World Cup '98 analysis (as summarised): request rates rose 5–10x before each scheduled game. This is a close analogue for scheduled-release surges. — cited via [Chen & Heidemann](https://ant.isi.edu/~johnh/PAPERS/Chen03b.pdf)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Wendell & Freedman, "Going Viral: Flash Crowds in an Open CDN", IMC 2011, using four years of CoralCDN logs. — [IMC 2011 PDF](https://conferences.sigcomm.org/imc/2011/docs/p549.pdf)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Jung, Krishnamurthy & Rabinovich, "Flash Crowds and Denial of Service Attacks: Characterization and Implications for CDNs and Web Sites" (WWW 2002). — [PDF](http://nms.lcs.mit.edu/papers/flash-crowds02.pdf)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Ari, Hong, Miller et al., "Managing Flash Crowds on the Internet" (MASCOTS 2003). — [PDF](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/~elm/Papers/mascots03flash.pdf)

### Inferences
- The absence of any OSS benchmark harness for result portals supports releasing the thesis's replay harness and the four reference implementations as an artifact contribution.
- The World Cup '98 trace (scheduled events, 5–10x pre-event ramp) is a public, citable workload that could validate or calibrate the thesis's synthetic result-day surge.
